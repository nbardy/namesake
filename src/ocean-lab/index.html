A tiling first. An ocean second.

The starting point is an Ammann–Beenker tiling: squares and 45° rhombi with eightfold order and no repeating translation. Its geometry becomes the wave shapes, rather than a grid placed over a water effect.

01 / STRUCTURE

Four dimensions → two

Integer points in four dimensions are projected into the plane. An octagonal window in the remaining dimensions selects which tiles exist: long-range order without an ordinary repeat.

02 / SHAPE

A crest has two owners

Compact twists and reversible shears bend boundaries and interiors together. Neighbors reuse the same sampled curve. The shapes change continuously while adjacency survives.

03 / OCTAVES

The silver ratio carries through

The tiling’s scale factor, δ = 1 + √2, sets fractal frequencies and foam sizes. Detail rotates by 45° between scales. Large currents and small capillaries share one coordinate field.

The construction and its guarantees

For j = 0…3, project n ∈ ℤ⁴ with physical directions eⱼ and perpendicular directions fⱼ. Keep points whose perpendicular image falls inside the regular octagon W. Elementary lattice faces become the two tile types.

eⱼ = (cos(jπ/4), sin(jπ/4))
fⱼ = (cos(3jπ/4), sin(3jπ/4))
x = Σ nⱼeⱼ,   u = Σ nⱼfⱼ
a = (1 + √2)/2
W: |uₓ| < a, |uᵧ| < a, |uₓ| + |uᵧ| < √2a

A compact polar twist rotates a point around m while preserving its radius. Its angle and first two derivatives vanish at the support boundary. Negating the angle gives the inverse; the Jacobian determinant is one.

r = |x − m|
θ(r) = A(1 − r²/ρ²)³ for r < ρ, otherwise 0
T(x) = m + Rθ(r)(x − m)
H(r) = −Aρ²/8 · (1 − r²/ρ²)⁴ inside the disk
J(x,y) = (−y,x)
T = time-one flow of J∇H,   det DT = 1

Sₓ(x,y) = (x + α sin(1.2y + 0.4), y)
Sᵧ(X,y) = (X, y + β sin(1.05X − 0.6))
F = Sᵧ ∘ Sₓ ∘ (star twists) ∘ (edge twists)

Composition stays invertible and area preserving, including when supports overlap. The same F transforms boundaries and material coordinates. A greedy matching on the dual graph gives each tile at most one strong shared crest.

The octave choice is tied to an integer inflation identity: P∥M = δP∥ and P⊥M = (1 − √2)P⊥. M alone is not a complete face-substitution rule.

This finite fractal field is a visual construction. The CPU reference normalizes octave weights; the ocean material uses a fixed amplitude scale. Coupling its scales to δ does not prove exact substitution compatibility of the decoration. The deformation generally changes metric eightfold symmetry.

The new shape family and its group structure

The curved shapes and ocean decoration are original combinations over established aperiodic geometry. A fixed-center twist is a one-parameter group: Tₐ ∘ Tᵦ = Tₐ₊ᵦ. Twists in disjoint disks commute. Overlapping twists can give different shapes when their order changes—a concrete way to search a larger family of fitting shapes.

Periodic Euclidean patterns have 17 wallpaper-group types; eightfold order belongs to the quasiperiodic setting. This is a new deformation family, not a claim of an eighteenth wallpaper group or a proof of a new finite aperiodic prototile set. The deformation can produce infinitely many curved instances.
