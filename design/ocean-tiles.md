# Ocean tile direction

The accepted starting point is the navy and turquoise ocean field from `81a0c3f`.
Preserve its large currents, branching foam, palette, and calm dark passages.
Make the tessellation readable as individual interlocking tiles at normal viewing size.
Use thin ink seams with a restrained illuminated lip, rather than wide grout.
Give neighboring tiles distinct categorical fine textures, not independent random colors.
Keep the same coarse ocean field flowing through every tile beneath those differences.
Use four related material families: each stays within navy, turquoise, and ivory.
Keep bright foam selective; broad dark and middle-value water provide quiet areas.
The opening panel is the existing hexagon scene, with Nicholas Bardy and navigation above it.
Keep the name readable and preserve the five established visual selectors and site navigation.
Inspect the real homepage on desktop and phone, plus the lab at full size and thumbnail.

## Mathematical contract

The underlying squares and rhombi are an Ammann–Beenker cut-and-project patch.
The same invertible, area-preserving map transforms the tiles and ocean coordinates.
Canonical shared boundaries are evaluated once and reused by both neighbors.
Four labels color the face-adjacency graph, so every shared edge separates two families.
The finite connected face graph has maximum degree four and a boundary of lower degree.
Peeling vertices of degree at most three produces an ordering for a proper four-coloring.
Classifications are deterministic for the same patch and stable under index permutation;
no claim is made that a different finite patch radius retains identical labels.

The coarse field is common across the entire plane. Category-dependent fine texture
and palette intentionally change at the seams. Only the shared coarse field and tile
geometry are described as continuous; the complete material is intentionally piecewise.
